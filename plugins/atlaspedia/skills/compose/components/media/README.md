# Media — photographs and embedded video, 3D models, PDFs

Use when a real object matters (hardware, boards, environments, demonstrations), or when a lecture, a 3D model, a datasheet/standard PDF or an external interactive explains better than text; add hotspots when the parts of a photo need labels, and a compare slider when two views of one thing differ in a way the reader should see by sliding. Every photo opens full size in a lightbox. Snippets: `photo.html`, `photo-hotspots.html`, `compare.html`, `embed-youtube.html`, `embed-model.html`, `embed-pdf.html`.

## Photographs and external images — `photo.html`

Shape: `<figure class="photo"><img src="…" alt="…" loading="lazy"><figcaption><b>Figure n.m — Caption.</b> <span class="credit">Source: <a href="…">Site, Author</a>, Licence</span></figcaption></figure>`. Rules: only URLs the dossier lists as **verified** (`dossier/00-overview.md` §5 or your module file's media table with a `✓`); always `alt`, caption, licence and a source link. Offline or on link rot, app.js swaps the image for a fallback box with the source link. **Use when** a real object matters. **Avoid** decorative stock images and any URL that is not in the dossier; draw an SVG when a diagram says more.

## Embedded media — `embed-youtube.html`, `embed-model.html`, `embed-pdf.html`

Shape: `<figure class="embed" data-kind="…" data-src="…" data-title="…"><figcaption>… <span class="credit">Source: <a href="…">…</a></span></figcaption></figure>`. `data-kind` ∈ `youtube` (ID or URL; served from youtube-nocookie with a thumbnail poster) · `vimeo` · `video` (direct .mp4/.webm, local or remote; optional `data-poster`) · `model` (.glb/.gltf via Google `<model-viewer>`, loaded on demand; `data-poster` optional) · `sketchfab` (model URL or id) · `pdf` (opens the browser PDF viewer) · `iframe` (any embeddable page: simulators, Desmos, GeoGebra, Observable). Everything is **click-to-load** (offline-safe, no third-party requests until the reader opts in); `data-autoload` loads immediately. Failed loads show a fallback with the source link. The `vimeo`, `video`, `sketchfab` and `iframe` kinds use the same markup as the three snippets with the kind and source changed.

**Use when** a lecture/talk explains better than text, a 3D model clarifies geometry, a datasheet/standard PDF is the reference, or an external interactive exists that you should not re-implement. **Avoid** IDs/URLs the dossier did not verify (YouTube ids are verified through the oEmbed check in the research protocol), autoplay, and embedding as a substitute for explanation — the caption must say what to look for and at which minute.

## Hotspots and lightbox — `photo-hotspots.html` (C10)

Shape: `<figure class="photo" data-hotspots><img …><ul class="hotspots"><li data-x="52" data-y="20"><b>Label</b> — what it is.</li>…</ul><figcaption>…</figcaption></figure>` — the photo rules above still apply (verified URL, `alt`, caption, licence, source link). `data-x`/`data-y` are percent of the picture from its left and top edges (0..100, decimals allowed); the bold text before the dash is the pin's label. The runtime wraps the picture in `div.hotspot-box`, places one numbered `button.hotspot` per item at (x %, y %) and shows the item's HTML in a `div.hotspot-tip` on hover, focus or click (click again, Escape or a click on the picture closes it); the list stays below as the numbered legend, and hovering a legend entry lights its pin. The box hugs the picture, so the percentages stay right when the image is letterboxed. **Lightbox:** every `figure.photo` gets a zoom-in cursor and a `⤢` button; clicking the picture or the button opens `div.lightbox[role=dialog]` with the image at full size and its caption; `×`, Escape or a click outside closes it, `←`/`→` walk the page's photos. `data-nolightbox` on the figure opts out. A picture that failed to load (the offline fallback box) never opens and shows no pins. **Use when** the reader must find parts on a real picture — connectors on a board, controls on an instrument, features of a specimen or a map sheet. **Avoid** more than about eight pins (draw a labelled SVG instead), pins on parts the caption does not need, and decorative photos.

## Compare slider — `compare.html` (C11)

Shape: `<figure class="compare" data-label-a="Before" data-label-b="After">` + exactly two panes (an `img`, an inline `svg` or a `div` each; a pane's own `data-label` overrides the figure's) + `figcaption` (+ an optional `.legend`). Both panes must have the same aspect ratio. The runtime moves them into `div.compare-box`, clips pane B at a divider the reader drags (a full-size `input[type=range].compare-range`: drag, click to jump, `←`/`→` when focused), draws the handle and puts the labels in the top corners. A photo pane that fails to load becomes the offline fallback box. **Use when** two views of the same thing differ in one way worth seeing by sliding — before/after, the same region under two projections, a sketch against the photo, a simulation against a measurement. **Avoid** two unrelated pictures (use two photos), panes of different shapes, and a comparison that a table states better (numbers).

## Validator messages this module can trigger

`photo without alt text` · `photo without a source link in .credit` · `embed without data-src` · `hotspot without data-x/data-y` (both attributes on every `li` of `ul.hotspots`) · `hotspot data-x 140 outside 0..100` (and `data-y`) · `hotspot data-x "left" is not a number` · `photo <id> has data-hotspots but no ul.hotspots` · `compare figure <id> has n panes, needs 2` (the caption and the legend do not count).

**H6 media manifest** — `build_site_data.py` lists every `figure.photo` (kind `hotspots` when it carries `data-hotspots`), `figure.compare` and `figure.embed` in `assets/media.json` (`page, id, kind, src, alt, title, caption, credit, creditUrl, licence`); the licence is parsed from the `.credit` text after the source link (`CC BY-SA 4.0`, `CC0`, `Public domain`, `© …`, `All rights reserved`), so write it exactly as the dossier's media table gives it. `validate.py` warns `media without a licence: <page> <id>` for photos, compare panes and direct video/model files; platform embeds (youtube, vimeo, sketchfab, pdf, iframe) are listed with `licence: null` and never warned, since the host's terms govern them.
